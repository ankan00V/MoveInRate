1 · Where the rating stands

A 4.6 average with one in four reviewers giving 1★

The store score is held up by people who tap five stars and leave. The people who take the time to write are far less happy, and their reviews are what a new user reads first.

Store rating4.64114,849 ratings · 1M+ installs
Written reviews, last 12 mo3.83★2,857 reviews · 24% are 1★
"Most relevant" reviews1.4★avg of top 40 shown on the store page; 37 of 40 are 1–2★
Developer reply rate3%last 12 mo · was 92% of 1★ reviews in 2022–24

What this means. The silent majority is happy. The rating is pulled down by a vocal group who are angry enough to leave the app, open the Play Store and type. Their triggers are specific and repeated, so fixing those triggers moves the number.

Competitor check. Safetrax Commuter shows 4.72★. Its written reviews are actually worse than ours (2.9★, 46% 1★ in the last 12 months), but it replies to 98% of them and gets far fewer written reviews. MoveInSync's experience is not worse than the market; its rating management is.

2 · Why

Most negative reviews are about the app getting in the way, not the cab

I classified every review from the last 12 months into 17 themes. The negative reviews (1–2★, n = 772) break down like this. One review can carry more than one theme.

Who owns the fix

Of negative reviews that name a cause, 84% involve the app: 48% app only plus 11% app and ops, out of the 71% that give any detail. Only 12% are purely about the ride itself: late cabs, routing, driver behaviour. The app is the main lever.

Rising in the last 30 days

"Not installed from Play Store" lockouts: 9.3% of negative reviews, up from 2.0%.
Forced rating and prompts: 8.0%, up from 3.0%.
Notification spam: 6.7%, up from 2.8%.
Driver behaviour: 4.0%, up from 1.3%. Routed to ops.

Releases are a repeat cause

Three releases in the last 12 months pushed the 1★ share at least 8 points above the 90 days before them: 12.13.1 (31% vs 22%), 12.14.0 (37% vs 22%) and 12.16.1 (34% vs 24%). The current version, 12.19.0, is at 32% against a 25% baseline, just under the line. A staged rollout gated on these signals would have caught each of them at 10% of users.

3 · What to do, in order

Ten tickets, ranked by rating impact per week of effort

Each ticket is written so a team can pick it up tomorrow. The evidence counts are from the last 12 months of negative reviews unless stated otherwise. Quotes are verbatim from public reviews.

P0 · THIS WEEKSmall code or configuration changes. Stops new damage.
1

Make trip feedback skippable and stop sending annoyed users to the store

App~3 days
Evidence
Forced rating, review nags and policy re-accept screens: 30 reviews, rising from 3% to 8% of negatives in the last 30 days. Users say outright that the prompt is why they left 1★. One reviewer also reports the in-app rating offers only 4 or 5 stars.
"forcing me to give rating after every ride, so I came here to give you 1 star. because if I don't rate, I can't use the app as there is no skip option." 1★ · v12.19.0 · Oct 2026
"users are only given options like 4 or 5 stars, with no way to rate 1, 2, or even 3" 2★ · v12.15.0 · Jul 2026
Fix
  • Add Skip to the trip-feedback screen. Never let it block tracking, OTP or QR boarding. Ask at most once a day.
  • Ask for a Play rating only through Google's In-App Review API, after a trip that completed on time with no complaint raised. Cap it at once per 60 days per user.
  • Remove any pre-question such as "would you rate us 5★?". Show all five stars in trip feedback. Google's guidelines forbid asking questions before the review card, and a 4–5-only picker is a policy risk to audit now.
  • Show the transport-policy acceptance only when the policy version changes, not on every launch.
Measure
Forced-prompt complaints fall to near zero within 4 weeks. Star-only rating volume holds, because the good-moment prompt replaces the forced one.
2

Stop the false "not installed from Play Store" lockout

App~1 week
Evidence
18 reviews in 12 months, but 9.3% of all negative reviews in the last 30 days, up from 2.0%. Every one is a user fully locked out until they reinstall, often right after an auto-update.
"in last 3 weeks I uninstall an installed it for 10 times due to the same error" 2★ · Aug 2026
"Auto updates itself and then the app does not launch. The only solution is to uninstall and reinstall" 1★ · v12.16.0
Fix
  • Likely cause: an installer-source or Play Integrity check that hard-blocks on a non-pass verdict. That includes transient errors and the window right after an update. Confirm with the verdict breakdown in Play Console and client logs.
  • Fail open on errors and "unevaluated" verdicts. Retry with backoff. Never block the "today's trip" view. Restrict only sensitive actions if needed.
  • Log every verdict with app version and device model so a spike is visible within a day.
Measure
Theme back below 1% of negative reviews. Zero hard blocks on error verdicts.
3

Reply to every 1–3★ review within 24 hours, with a real answer

Customer Success + FDEstart now, ~1 hr/day
Evidence
The reply rate fell from 92–98% to 3% in July 2024. The 40 "most relevant" reviews that greet every store visitor average 1.4★, and 25 of them have no reply. Earlier replies were templates that redirected users ("reach out to your transport facility manager"), and the 1★ share did not improve while they ran. Google reports that users who get a reply raise their rating by 0.7★ on average.
Fix
  • Start with the 40 "most relevant" reviews this week.
  • Use the weekly monitor's triage queue. Every negative review is pre-tagged with its theme and owner. App issues get a specific answer or a known-issue status. Ride issues go to that client's transport desk with a reference number.
  • Close the loop. When a fix ships, reply again ("fixed in 12.x, please update") so the reviewer can revise their rating.
Measure
At least 90% of 1–3★ reviews answered within 48 hours. "Most relevant" average above 2.5★ within a quarter. Track rating changes on replied reviews in Play Console.
P1 · NEXT 2 SPRINTSEngineering work on the biggest themes.
4

Keep people signed in, and make OTP delivery reliable

App + Backend~2 sprints
Evidence
Login, OTP and forced logouts: 109 reviews (14% of negatives), with steady complaints since early 2025. The worst failures happen at the kerb, when the cab is there.
"when cab is waiting for you and you are in hurry, you will be logged out with no access to cab number/otp/call option" 2★ · v12.8.2
"I stepped in a no network zone and it logged me out" 1★ · v12.13.2
Fix
  • Use sliding refresh tokens (for example 30–90 days, per client security policy). Never log out because of a network failure or a failed refresh. Keep showing cached data and refresh quietly on reconnect.
  • For SSO clients, refresh silently instead of sending users to a full re-login.
  • Measure OTP delivery rate and latency by channel and mobile operator. Fall back automatically to WhatsApp or email, with a visible resend timer and a message saying where the code was sent.
Measure
Forced logins per active user per week down 80%. OTP delivery success above 98% within 30 seconds.
5

Open straight to a cached "today's trip" card

App~2 sprints
Evidence
Crashes, won't open, slow or blank screen is the largest theme: 173 reviews (22% of negatives). Related themes include trip details unavailable offline, the QR and OTP screen, and banners blocking launch.
"Loading and showing full screen banner on every app launch is terrible. I am opening the app to see my cab timing" 1★ · v12.11.2
"when you click view otp, it pops up the seat belt [check] first ... I should be able to see the otp in one click" 2★ · v12.19.0
Fix
  • Cache the current trip on the device: cab number, driver, OTP, pickup time and helpdesk number. Render it first on launch, before any network call.
  • Move banners, notices and campaign pop-ups off the launch path. Show the OTP in one tap, with the seat-belt check after boarding.
  • Track cold-start time, crash rate and ANR rate per release in Android Vitals. Google's bad-behaviour thresholds are 1.09% user-perceived crash rate and 0.47% ANR rate.
Measure
P90 time to trip card under 2 seconds. Crash and slow-launch theme below 12% of negatives.
6

Gate every release on review signals with a staged rollout

Release engineering~3 days
Evidence
Three regressions in 12 months: 12.13.1, 12.14.0 and 12.16.1, each 8–15 points above the prior 1★ baseline. The current release, 12.19.0, is 7 points above.
Fix
  • Roll out through Play Console in stages: 5%, then 20%, 50% and 100%, with at least 48 hours at each stage.
  • Halt automatically when the weekly monitor flags the version (1★ share at least 8 points over baseline with 20 or more reviews), or when Vitals crash or ANR rates breach their thresholds.
  • Add the top three negative themes for each new version to release notes and retros.
Measure
No release more than 5 points above baseline reaches 100% of users.

App + Backend

Make booking state impossible to misread

App
Evidence
Booking, cancel, waitlist and schedule: 102 reviews (13%). New UI or update made it harder: 48 (6%).
"Booking a shuttle is now a 2 step process previously it used to be 1. I got confused and thought I had booked a cab while I hadn't." 1★ · v12.14.1

Restore one-step booking. Show an explicit Booked or Waitlisted status with the cancellation cut-off inline. Label times with AM/PM. Allow editing a booking without cancelling it.

Fair no-shows and one-tap contact

AppOps
Evidence
Late cabs and disputed no-shows: 73 reviews (9.5%). Can't reach driver or helpdesk: 64 (8%).
"came half an hour earlier and marked no show at pick point no call" 1★ · v12.9.4

Mark "arrived" only from the driver's GPS geofence, not by hand. Show a visible wait timer, and allow a no-show only after the timer runs out and a call attempt is logged. 10

Send ride complaints to the people who can fix them

Ops + account teams
Evidence
About 23% of negative reviews are about the ride: late cabs, routing and clubbing, driver behaviour, safety. The app team can't fix these. They come from specific vendors and client sites, and reviewers often name their employer.
Fix
The monitor sends a weekly digest of ride-tagged reviews to ops and account managers. Any review mentioning safety or harassment gets same-day escalation, and every one is listed individually, never only in aggregate.

4 · Expected impact

Halving app-caused 1–2★ reviews passes the competitor

Google weights recent ratings, so I model a 12-month window. About 21% of ratings come with a written review. That puts last year's total at about 13,500 ratings, of which 2,857 were written (3.83★ average) and the rest were star-only taps (4.90★ average). This model gives 4.68 against the 4.64 the store shows, close enough to use for comparisons.

5 · Keeping the answer current

"How are our Play Store ratings?" now has a live answer

The brief says this question will come up again. So the analysis above is a small pipeline, not a one-off notebook. It runs every hour, costs nothing to operate, and gives each team its own view. This memo is dated; the live dashboard always shows the current numbers.

What it does every hour

  • Fetches new reviews and the store snapshot for every MoveInSync app, incrementally, plus the benchmark app.
  • Tags each review with theme and owner using a rule-based classifier. On a hand-checked holdout of 80 negative reviews, about 91% of tags were correct and about 88% of themes present were caught.
  • Alerts in Slack the moment something new appears, and flags three things: release regressions, themes rising sharply in the last 30 days, and safety mentions, listed one by one.
  • Publishes the live dashboard and a reply triage queue, and keeps a history of store-rating snapshots so the trend builds up over time.

Run it

pip install -r requirements.txt
python pipeline/run_weekly.py            # weekly summary
python pipeline/run_weekly.py --hourly   # new alerts only
# → data/metrics.json, dashboard/index.html,
#   data/triage_queue.csv

Live dashboard: ankan00v.github.io/MoveInRate. Code and data: github.com/ankan00V/MoveInRate. A GitHub Actions workflow checks the Play Store every hour, redeploys the dashboard and posts anything new to Slack. Every Monday at 07:00 IST it also posts the weekly summary and commits a data snapshot. If an LLM key is added, the rule-based tagger can be swapped for an LLM classifier with the same output columns.

6 · How I got here

Method, tools and limits

Data. I searched the Play Store for the developer "MoveInSync: Enabling Hybrid Workplaces" and found six apps. The employee app has 99% of the ratings, so the analysis focuses on it. I pulled every public review (24,314) with the open-source google-play-scraper library, along with the store histogram, the "most relevant" ordering a visitor sees, and the same data for Safetrax Commuter as a benchmark.

Analysis. I read about 400 recent 1–2★ reviews by hand to build the 17-theme taxonomy. I encoded it as English and Hinglish patterns, checked it against a separate holdout sample, and fixed the misses. I then cut the data by month, release version and owner, compared the store histogram with the written reviews to separate star-only raters, and modelled the rating scenarios.

Tools. Python (pandas, google-play-scraper). Claude Code as an AI pair for writing the scraper and pipeline, drafting regex patterns and building this page. Every number here comes from the pipeline output, and I made the theme definitions and priorities by reading the reviews myself. Google's Android developer documentation for the In-App Review API and Android Vitals thresholds.

Limits. Public reviews only. I could not see Play Console data (rating breakdown by version or device, Android Vitals, the Play Integrity verdict mix), iOS, or internal tickets, and the root causes for tickets 2 and 4 are hypotheses to confirm against those. Reviews from 2014 to early 2018 reflect a much older product and are excluded from the conclusions. The driver apps (Partner Lite 4.29★, Driver Partner 3.5★) matter too, because tracking and ETA depend on the driver's phone, but they have under 1% of the employee app's review volume and are out of scope here.

Data through 9 October 2026. Source: public Google Play reviews and listings (India, English). Reviews are quoted verbatim, without reviewer names.
